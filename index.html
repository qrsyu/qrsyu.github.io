Hi, I'm Shannon. I am a Ph.D. student in computational neuroscience working with Prof. David Heeger and Prof. Jonathan Winawer at New York University. My research interests lie in the computational cognitive neuroscience of human internal sensory experience, dreaming and consciousness.

A key question of my current research is how the brain generates visual experience from the inside, and what makes such experience feel "seen", or even real. Visual imagery, dreaming, and hallucinations are all examples of internally generated visual experience. They are vivid and perception-like but without external visual input. Understanding the circuit mechanisms that allow the brain to generate such experience is essential for understanding the reality experience of the mind and its relation to consciousness.

Besides, human spatiotemporal perception is another important aspect of reality experience. A long term goal for my research is to integrate the study of internal visual experience with spatiotemporal perception, to answer the metaphysical question: What is the nature of reality experience? I use theoretical modelling (e.g., dynamical systems) with neural and behavioural data to approach this. More detailed information about my research can be found on Research page.
